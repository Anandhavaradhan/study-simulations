How launch speed shapes the path

Below circular speed, the launch point becomes the farthest point (aphelion) and the planet dives in toward the Sun. Above it, the launch point is the closest point (perihelion) and the orbit swings out. At √2 vc the total energy reaches zero and the orbit opens into a parabola; faster still gives a hyperbola.

Notice that the slower inner orbits lap the faster outer ones: bigger orbits take longer, T² = a³.

1 · The ideas, in order

  1. Gravity is the only force. The Sun pulls the planet with acceleration a = −GM r̂ / r². The simulation steps this forward in tiny time steps (velocity-Verlet), smaller near the Sun where things change fast.
  2. Circular speed. For a circle, gravity supplies exactly the centripetal force: v²/r = GM/r², so v_c = √(GM/r). At 1 AU that is 2π AU/yr ≈ 29.8 km/s.
  3. Energy decides the family of orbit. Specific energy E = v²/2 − GM/r. E < 0 → closed ellipse (bound). E = 0 → parabola, which happens at v_esc = √(2GM/r) = √2·v_c. E > 0 → hyperbola (escapes).
  4. Launching sideways fixes the turning point. Because velocity starts perpendicular to the radius, the launch point is either perihelion (v > v_c) or aphelion (v < v_c). The eccentricity is e = |v²r/GM − 1| and the semi-major axis is a = r / (2 − v²r/GM).
  5. Kepler's laws appear in the graphs. Angular momentum is conserved (r·v⊥ = constant), so the planet is fastest at perihelion: watch |v| spike in the velocity graph. Period follows T² = a³ (T in years, a in AU).

2 · Exam questions

Q1. A planet at 1 AU is launched perpendicular to the Sun–planet line at 1.2 times circular speed. Find e, a, aphelion distance and period.

v²r/GM = (1.2)² = 1.44 since v_c² = GM/r.

Eccentricity: e = |1.44 − 1| = 0.44. Since v > v_c, the launch point is perihelion, r_p = 1 AU.

Semi-major axis: a = r/(2 − 1.44) = 1/0.56 = 1.79 AU. Check: r_p = a(1 − e) = 1.786 × 0.56 = 1.00 AU ✓

Aphelion: r_a = a(1 + e) = 1.786 × 1.44 = 2.57 AU.

Period (Kepler III, yr–AU units): T = a^{3/2} = 1.786^{1.5} = 2.39 years.

Q2. Derive the escape speed from distance r and show it equals √2 times the circular speed. Evaluate it at 1 AU.

To escape, the planet must reach r → ∞ with v ≥ 0, so total energy must be at least zero: ½mv² − GMm/r ≥ 0.

Minimum case: ½v_esc² = GM/r ⇒ v_esc = √(2GM/r).

Circular speed is v_c = √(GM/r), so v_esc/v_c = √2 ⇒ v_esc = √2·v_c.

At 1 AU: v_c = 29.8 km/s ⇒ v_esc = 1.414 × 29.8 ≈ 42.1 km/s (8.89 AU/yr). The resulting path is a parabola (e = 1).

Q3. For the orbit in Q1, find the planet's speed at aphelion. Which conservation law do you use and why?

Gravity acts along the line to the Sun, so it exerts no torque about the Sun: angular momentum is conserved.

At perihelion and aphelion velocity is perpendicular to r, so m v_p r_p = m v_a r_a.

v_p = 1.2 × 29.8 = 35.7 km/s, r_p = 1 AU, r_a = 2.57 AU.

v_a = v_p r_p / r_a = 35.7 / 2.57 ≈ 13.9 km/s.

Energy check: v_a² − v_p² should equal 2GM(1/r_a − 1/r_p); both give the same result, confirming the answer.

Q4. In the simulation, x(t) is a smooth sine wave for a circular orbit but becomes lopsided with sharp velocity spikes for an eccentric orbit. Explain.

Circular orbit: speed and angular speed ω are constant, so x = r cos(ωt), y = r sin(ωt) — pure sinusoids, and |v| is a flat line.

Elliptical orbit: by Kepler's second law (equal areas in equal times, from conservation of angular momentum) the planet sweeps quickly past perihelion and crawls near aphelion.

So x(t) spends a long time near the aphelion value (broad, flat extreme) and swings rapidly through perihelion (narrow, sharp extreme).

|v| peaks sharply at perihelion and has a broad minimum at aphelion; vₓ and v_y flip sign quickly at perihelion, producing spikes.

Q5. A planet at 1 AU has speed 7.0 AU/yr. Is it bound? State the orbit type with a calculation.

GM = 4π² ≈ 39.48 AU³/yr². E = v²/2 − GM/r = 49/2 − 39.48 = 24.5 − 39.48 = −14.98 AU²/yr².

E < 0, so the planet is bound and moves on an ellipse.

Also v_c = 6.28 < 7.0 < v_esc = 8.89 AU/yr, so the launch point is perihelion; e = 49/39.48 − 1 ≈ 0.24.

3 · Key points to remember

  • v_c = √(GM/r); at 1 AU, 29.8 km/s = 2π AU/yr.
  • v_esc = √2·v_c ≈ 42.1 km/s at 1 AU.
  • v < v_c → launch point is aphelion; v_c < v < v_esc → launch point is perihelion.
  • E < 0 ellipse, E = 0 parabola, E > 0 hyperbola.
  • Sideways launch: e = |v²r/GM − 1|, a = r/(2 − v²r/GM).
  • Angular momentum conserved → fastest at perihelion (Kepler II).
  • T² = a³ in years and AU (Kepler III); bigger orbits are slower.
  • Tilting the launch velocity tilts the orbit plane but not its shape.
